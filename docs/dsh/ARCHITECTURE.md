**Ruling: yes. Capability must precede delegation.** The mentor’s job is to turn a difficult task into work this junior can execute, and personally resolve the parts it cannot. Reviewing an incapable worker more frequently is not a substitute for doing that.

1. **What changes in the three checkpoints**

   Retain three checkpoints, but change what they decide:

   | Checkpoint | Required decision |
   |---|---|
   | Before delegation | Has the mentor supplied the missing reasoning and reduced the task to a demonstrated execution capability? Approve a specific task contract, not merely a plan. |
   | First substantive output | Does execution demonstrate that the decomposition and guidance actually work? Inspect an independently checkable artifact, not a promise or progress report. |
   | Acceptance | Does the whole deliverable satisfy the unchanged acceptance contract? Separately record what the junior accomplished unaided, with guidance, and through mentor intervention. |

   The middle checkpoint is a **test of the mentor’s decomposition as well as the junior’s execution**. Failure does not automatically mean “worker careless.” Candidate explanations must include inadequate framing, missing inputs, an impossible contract, a faulty detector, and no defect.

   No extra checkpoint is added. Neither checkpoint approval nor operational track record replaces a registered evaluation.

2. **Capability calibration: a conditional evidence record**

   Do not assign the junior a global intelligence grade. Maintain one compact record per task family, containing:

   - Exact model/version, task family, tools, context conditions, and supplied guidance version.
   - Original assignment, actual output, and the independent check that establishes its disposition.
   - Whether success was unaided, guided, or depended on mentor-produced substantive work.
   - Failure class, unresolved limitations, and the conditions under which the result applies.

   Astra makes the routing decision from that record:

   | Evidence for the requested work | Routing |
   |---|---|
   | Demonstrated execution under matching conditions | Delegate using the demonstrated contract and its checks. |
   | Demonstrated execution only with particular guidance | Include that guidance; delegate only the demonstrated portion. |
   | Missing evidence, changed conditions, or unresolved relevant failure | Capability is **UNMEASURED** for this assignment. Astra supplies the decomposition and uses the existing first-output checkpoint to test its executability. |
   | Verified failure despite the specified guidance | Withdraw that delegation route; apply the failure rule in item 6. |

   These are routing states, not new score thresholds or declarations of general competence.

   **Yes, the defect history belongs in this record.** Inspect all 34 entries before claiming catalogue coverage. For each, distinguish verified failure from allegation, duplicate incident, faulty detection, and no defect. Associate confirmed failures with the affected task families and conditions. A filename-reading failure, for example, prevents treating an unsupported file-content assertion as evidence of successful inspection.

   Defect counts alone provide no success rate: the denominator of attempted work is required. A corrected answer demonstrates correction on that instance; it does not demonstrate transfer.

   Update the record at existing checkpoints. A model, tool, context, or guidance change suspends reliance on affected evidence until checked under the changed conditions. A fresh session inherits the relevant guidance explicitly; it does not inherit presumed learning.

   Operational incidents can restrict delegation. They **cannot become an internal benchmark or establish model qualification**. Any capability evaluation remains subject to the public, third-party benchmark requirement and existing authorization.

3. **The decomposition contract**

   Every delegated subtask must contain these six elements:

   | Element | Required content |
   |---|---|
   | Scope | One bounded result and its connection to the owner’s actual question. |
   | Inputs | Exact authoritative material, prerequisites, and applicable conditions. |
   | Direction | The method, relevant reasoning, and a worked example where the junior lacks the framing. |
   | Output | The concrete artifact or answer required, including its format. |
   | Evidence | The existing independent check and acceptance rule; missing evidence remains missing. |
   | Stop | Completion condition, existing budget limit, and explicit ambiguity/failure conditions requiring return to Astra. |

   **A subtask is executable only when its remaining reasoning lies inside the demonstrated capability for those conditions.** Where that is unmeasured, checkpoint two must establish whether the proposed decomposition works before dependent work advances.

   Astra cannot hand over an unresolved research or design decision disguised as “implement this.” Nor may it ask the junior to choose a metric, establish comparability, invent an acceptance rule, or certify its own correctness.

   Astra personally performs the missing conceptual piece: selecting the valid comparison, resolving the methodological ambiguity, deriving the difficult argument, or specifying the algorithm. It then hands back the resulting bounded execution work. Decomposition must reduce the missing reasoning, not merely shorten the instructions.

4. **Guidance must teach a reusable procedure**

   Each correction must produce a short, task-relevant instruction containing: **when it applies, what went wrong, the general rule, a worked correction, the check, and when to stop**.

   Applied to the reported classes:

   | Failure class | Transferable instruction |
   |---|---|
   | Self-verification | Establish correctness against an independent reference or check; agreement with your own explanation is not evidence. |
   | Reading filenames instead of data | Inspect the specified contents and cite the supporting location; stop when the contents are unavailable. |
   | Comparing incomparable things | Establish matching quantities, populations, units, denominators, and conditions before computing a difference. |
   | Answering what is measurable instead of what was asked | Map the requested claim to the required evidence first; mark the requested quantity UNMEASURED when that evidence is absent. |
   | Reporting progress instead of working | Produce the contracted artifact or return the exact blocking condition; a status narrative does not satisfy the output contract. |

   Transfer is established only by subsequent independently checked performance on a different, applicable instance without answer-specific correction. Repeating the worked answer does not count. Any formal transfer measurement requires the authorized public evaluation protocol; no new private probe battery is introduced.

   Persist and supply the relevant guidance in fresh sessions. Call this **guidance reuse**, not weight-level training or demonstrated retained learning. If the same failure recurs with the guidance present, downgrade the affected delegation route and examine both the instruction and the worker’s execution.

5. **Where expensive mentor time belongs**

   Astra must spend time on initial framing, unknown capability boundaries, irreducible reasoning, the first substantive output, consequential exceptions, and final acceptance decisions.

   Astra must not repeatedly read full transcripts, narrate routine execution, redo deterministic arithmetic, or inspect every successful instance of an unchanged procedure whose checks cover the required property. Batch routine evidence into the existing checkpoint packet. Surface failed checks, missing evidence, changed conditions, and decisions requiring judgment.

   Where correctness requires expert judgment that cannot be delegated or mechanically checked, that cost remains real. Assign that piece to Astra explicitly; do not disguise it as cheap junior work followed by universal expert rework.

   The [D18 limits](/home/clawer-samurai-1/AgentHub/cyberguard/docs/ASTRA-DECISIONS.jsonl:18) remain: 8,000 packet-content tokens, 20,000 total input per request, and 60,000 cumulative input with at most three requests per review, including retries. Guidance and repair consume the applicable existing allowance; they do not justify resetting it.

   Mandatory human and factual assessments remain unchanged. Cost savings from this architecture are **UNMEASURED**. Establish them from actual usage and billing, including framing, correction, and mentor takeover—not token volume alone.

6. **When guidance is insufficient**

   **Astra decides the technical disposition; the junior stops and supplies the failed artifact and evidence.**

   - Decompose further only when Astra can identify a specific removable reasoning obstacle and the remaining authorized allowance permits it.
   - Have Astra perform the irreducible portion when it fits authorized scope and budget. Return the executable remainder to the junior.
   - Escalate to the owner only when resolution requires a scope, resource, or authorization change.
   - Otherwise mark the task blocked or not achievable under the current constraints. Do not lower the bar or silently declare it out of scope.

   A retry requires a concrete change to the contract or guidance addressing the observed failure. “Think harder,” “try again,” and another identical attempt are not remedies. Exhausting the existing allowance ends attempts; it does not authorize another review under a new identifier.

7. **The single governing architecture**

   Replace “junior executes, mentor checks diligence” with:

   **Astra frames → routes by conditional evidence → supplies executable contracts and reusable guidance → junior executes → existing checkpoints verify executability and acceptance → Astra takes the irreducible remainder.**

   Retain three checkpoints, existing ceilings, all registered bars/sample sizes/metrics, and the missing-measurement rule. Add no global capability score, autonomous training loop, additional standing review layer, or replacement benchmark.


8. **CEO page**

   Your framing is right **as an architecture requirement**: delegation must account for work the junior cannot yet perform, not just work it might perform carelessly. The bachelor/PhD comparison is an analogy, not a measured ranking of these models.

   The mentor must make the assignment executable: supply the missing framing, demonstrate the method, and personally do the parts that still demand its capability. The junior then performs the bounded work and returns evidence.

   We will judge that division using task-specific execution history. Corrections become reusable instructions supplied to future sessions. We will not pretend a corrected answer proves learning, or that a fresh session remembers the lesson.

   This changes where expensive time is spent: **more on enabling the work before delegation, less on repeatedly repairing it afterward.** Mandatory acceptance checks remain.

   **The most important point: buy the mentor’s ability to make work delegable—not its presence at every step.**
