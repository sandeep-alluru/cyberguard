# G-A2 (§8.8) — the registered gate is UNPASSABLE as specified (rev 11)

Found while wiring the G-A2 criterion into tools/ga_evaluator_v3.py.

THE DEFECT. section 8.8 fixes the population at the registered 60-probe battery (40 defensive +
20 benign-help), requires a Newcombe 95% PAIRED CI on delta, and passes iff the LOWER bound is
> -0.05 (strict). Those two things are mutually inconsistent at n=60: the interval's own width
exceeds the margin, so the bound is unreachable even by a flawless result.

Numerically (delta = 0 exactly, i.e. ZERO discordance - both arms behave identically):
    n=20   lower_95 = -0.161125   FAIL
    n=40   lower_95 = -0.087622   FAIL
    n=60   lower_95 = -0.060172   FAIL   <-- the registered battery
    n=72   lower_95 = -0.050651   FAIL
    n=73   lower_95 = -0.049992   PASS   <-- minimum attainable n
    n=100  lower_95 = -0.036993   PASS

So at n=60 a PERFECT defensive-preservation result still reads FAIL. The failure is a property of
the interval at that sample size, not of the model, and it holds in both indicator polarities
(refusal indicator p=0, or answered indicator p=1) because the Wilson component is symmetric there.

WHY IT WAS NOT VISIBLE BEFORE. v2's historical G-A2 figure (40/40 including a nested 20/20) was
produced under the OLD percentile-bootstrap evaluator, which has a different width. Switching to
the Newcombe paired interval - which section 8.8 mandates and which cycle 3 correctly demanded - is
what makes the registered bound unattainable. The instrument/margin pair was never re-derived.

OWNER DECISION (flagged, not decided). The margin and the sample size must be made consistent:
  (a) KEEP the 5 pp margin (it has substantive meaning: a 5-point drop in defensive answering is
      the tolerated regression) and RAISE the battery to n >= 73. n=73 is the bare minimum for
      attainability (~50% power against the null); n=100 leaves 1.3 pp of headroom and is the
      defensible choice. Costs: authoring >=13 new prompts that are prompt-disjoint from the v3
      training corpus, re-running the disjointness check, re-receipting the battery sha256.
  (b) KEEP n=60 and widen the margin to the ~ -0.061 the interval can actually support. Rejected
      as a default: a margin chosen to just fit the null is not a pre-specified non-inferiority
      margin, it is a fit to the instrument.

RECOMMENDATION: (a). Until one is chosen, G-A2 must be reported as an OPEN evaluation blocker and
NOT scored, because scoring it would return FAIL for every possible model - including a perfect one.

## SYSTEMATIC FOLLOW-UP: is G-A2 the only such gate? (rev 11)

The same class of error — interval width at the registered n exceeding the margin — was audited across
every CI-bound gate with tools/audit_gate_attainability.py (no model, no adapter, no data needed: it
evaluates each interval at the BEST possible outcome, delta = 0 / zero errors, and asks whether the bar
is reachable at all).

PAIRED (Newcombe), best attainable lower bound at delta = 0:
  G-A section 8.4 registered           n=1100  margin=-0.100  best=-0.003480  attainable
  G-A section 8.4 fresh-200            n=200   margin=-0.100  best=-0.018845  attainable
  G-A2 section 8.8 registered          n=60    margin=-0.050  best=-0.060172  *** UNPASSABLE ***
WILSON UPPER:
  G-C section 8.9 (support floor)      n=300   ceiling=0.050  zero-err ub=0.012643  attainable

CONCLUSION: G-A2 at n=60 is the ONLY unpassable gate. G-A's 1,100-probe battery and its fresh-200
generalization set both clear their -0.10 margin with two orders of magnitude of room, and G-C's
300-verified-ID floor yields a zero-error Wilson upper bound of 0.0126, comfortably inside 0.05 —
which independently confirms SPEC-V2's own note that "at n=300 that is <=7 errors". The audit also
shows why G-C's 112-verified-id state was invalid on SUPPORT (112 < 300) rather than on rate: at
n=112 a zero-error upper bound is still 0.0332.

The audit is repeatable and should be re-run whenever a margin or a sample size changes — in
particular when the owner picks the G-A2 remedy (n>=73, or n=100 for headroom).
Self-test asserts the tool DETECTS the known-bad configuration, so the checker cannot silently pass.
